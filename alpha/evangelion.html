02

HIDEAKI ANNO · 1995

NEON GENESIS
EVANGELION

Giant bio-mechanical robots fighting incomprehensible angels. Underneath it: three teenagers who've each concluded, separately, that they're impossible to love.

NERV
THE PREMISE

The Angels Keep Coming

Fourteen-year-old Shinji Ikari is summoned by the father who abandoned him to pilot Eva Unit-01, a towering bio-mechanical weapon, against beings called Angels that are trying to end humanity for reasons the show barely bothers to explain. He's joined, eventually, by Asuka and Rei — two other teenagers, each broken in a completely different, completely specific way.

On the surface it's a mecha show: robots, kaiju-scale threats, a secret organization called NERV running the defense. Watch three episodes and that surface starts to crack. The battles matter less than what happens in the cockpit's sync-ratio tests, the therapy sessions that aren't called therapy, the silences at the dinner table.

It's a show about the fear of being known. Anno has been open that he made it while depressed, and it reads that way — Shinji's refrain, "I mustn't run away," isn't a hero's mantra, it's a kid negotiating with his own instinct to disappear.

The famous "hedgehog's dilemma" — wanting closeness enough to risk the pain of it — is stated outright in the show and never resolved neatly, because the show doesn't believe it resolves neatly for anyone.

PRODUCTION

Made on a Collapsing Schedule

DIRECTOR
Hideaki Anno
STUDIO
Gainax
RUN
26 episodes + follow-up films (1995–1997, 2007–2021)
FORMAT
Weekly TV series, later reworked as theatrical "Rebuild" films
FINALE
Abstract, budget-strained, and one of the most argued-over endings in TV
RESURGENCE
2019 Netflix relicensing introduced it to a new generation worldwide
The original TV budget ran out before the story did — the last two episodes are famously built from still frames, sketches, and internal monologue instead of animation. It became one of the most debated finales ever made, arguably *because* of the constraint, not despite it.
LEGACY

It Gave the Genre Permission

MECHA

Permission to be sad. Before Eva, mecha anime were largely about heroism under pressure. After it, shows like Darling in the Franxx and even Hollywood's Pacific Rim treat the pilot's psychology as inseparable from the robot fights.

DISCOURSE

It normalized talking about depression, dissociation, and self-worth inside genre fiction aimed at teenagers — years before that became common in Western YA or streaming TV.

DESIGN

NERV's control-room aesthetic — angular red-on-black UI, hexagonal warning displays — has been repeated in games and films chasing a "high-tech command center" look ever since.

2019

Its Netflix relicensing sparked an entirely new fandom cycle, proving a 25-year-old show could still dominate conversation the week it dropped.
