Crimson Desert — Guide for Skyrim Veterans

Pearl Abyss · Released March 19, 2026 · PC · Open World Action RPG

Complete Guide Hard Combat Open World ~50–80 hrs 🗡️ 8.5 / 10

Crimson Desert is Pearl Abyss's massive open-world action RPG set in the land of Pywel — roughly twice the size of Skyrim and larger than Red Dead Redemption 2's map. It's stunning to explore, brutal in combat, and built very differently from anything Bethesda ever made.

This guide is written for Skyrim veterans who have 100% completed Tamriel and want to know exactly what to expect — and more importantly, what assumptions to drop at the door.

🏙️ First Hour

Don't leave Hernand — your starting city

  • 1
    Spend real time in Hernand — this is your Whiterun Resist the urge to sprint into the wild. Talk to every named NPC, find the Butcher, Grocer, and Smithy, and grab every "favor" quest. These early tasks ease you into combat systems and reward you with gear and reputation before the world demands more of you.
  • 2
    Get through Chapter 4 before heavy free-roaming Large portions of the map start nearly empty. They unlock and come alive as you advance the main story through Chapter 4. This is the opposite of Skyrim's "ignore the main quest forever" approach — push the story first. The world rewards you massively when it opens up.
  • 3
    Use a controller — seriously The combat system was built with a controller in mind. Multiple reviewers describe mouse and keyboard as "very, very difficult" in combat. Grab your PS or Xbox controller from hour one and save yourself the frustration of relearning combat after the fact.
⚡ Progression — Abyss Artifacts

There are no levels here — here's what replaces them

  • 4
    Abyss Artifacts = Skyrim skill points + perk points combined There are no XP bars. You find glowing Abyss Artifacts throughout the world. They let you revive at 30% HP on death and invest points into stats and skills. Collect them religiously — they are the entire progression system and the key to surviving harder areas.
  • 5
    Question marks on the map hide fast travel points AND Artifacts Roughly 40% of map question marks are Abyss Nexus fast travel points. The rest are Abyss Cressets that reward Artifacts. Clear every single one — you unlock fast travel AND get stronger simultaneously. Think of this like your Word Wall hunt, except it also upgrades your actual stats.
  • 6
    Invest in Stamina early — it runs everything Stamina governs your ability to block, attack, sprint, and dodge. Early Abyss Artifact points into Stamina will feel immediately impactful in every combat encounter. Don't neglect it chasing damage stats in the opening hours.
🌍 Exploration & Combat

How to thrive in Pywel

  • 7
    Ring bells on tall structures to reveal the map Ringing bells on elevated structures unlocks the regional map with points of interest — Pywel's version of climbing a tower in any open-world game. Always go high. Always ring the bell. This is how you find Artifact locations, quests, and merchants before stumbling into them by accident.
  • 8
    Bosses will destroy you early — stockpile food Boss encounters are notoriously demanding early in the game. Hunt animals for meat, buy from the Butcher and Grocer in Hernand, and cook at Campfires. Bring as much food as you can into every boss fight. Food is your primary healing system.
  • 9
    Combat is about timing — not stat-checking Crimson Desert rewards reading enemy behavior and mastering the dodge, block, and counter system. A weapon that fits your rhythm will outperform a higher-stat weapon you haven't learned. Spend time in early fights learning patterns. Swinging first without reading an attack will get you punished.
  • 10
    Do commissions constantly for Silver and Contribution Rank Faction quests and commissions raise your Contribution Rank, which unlocks unique regional gear. This is your best income loop and the key to staying competitive on equipment throughout the game. Don't treat commissions as optional side content — they're your main progression outside of Artifacts.
🍲 The Sinigaming Verdict

Our take on Crimson Desert

  • ✅ Open-world exploration feels like a spiritual successor to Skyrim — and surpasses it in scale
  • ✅ Completionist instincts are perfectly suited here — enormous amount to find and track
  • ⚠️ Combat, progression, and fast travel are completely different — don't expect to adapt in the first few hours
  • ⚠️ Cinematic, focused story — you're playing Kliff's story, not writing your own
  • 💡 Get through Chapter 4 before going full explorer mode — the world opens up massively after
  • 💡 Estimated 50–80+ hours to see everything — your kind of game if you loved Skyrim's scope
