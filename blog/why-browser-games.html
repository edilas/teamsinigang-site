Why We Make Browser Games in 2026

Published May 2026 · By Team Sinigang

People ask us this all the time. Usually with a slight tilt of the head, like they're trying to figure out if we've missed something obvious. "Why browser games? Don't you want to put it on Steam? What about mobile apps?"

Fair questions. Here's our honest answer.

The Friction Problem

Every barrier between a person and a game costs you players. An app store page. A download. An account creation. A loading screen. A tutorial you have to sit through before you even know if you like what you're playing.

Browser games have none of that. You click a link and you're playing. That's it. On your phone, your old laptop, your work computer during lunch, your mum's tablet at Christmas. No install, no sign-up, no "storage full" error.

We believe in that frictionless moment. It's the same reason you'd pick up a book left open on a table before you'd download a reading app to read it. The path of least resistance matters enormously when you're talking about someone giving you 30 seconds of their attention.

The "Too Small for Steam" Problem

Be honest with yourself about the kind of games we make. Fingularity is a swipe-to-destroy casual game. Jungle Sprint is an endless runner. Puzzle Pot is a food-themed puzzle game. These are not 60-hour RPGs. They're not going to find their audience at $4.99 on Steam — and charging for them would feel wrong when the whole point is accessibility.

The browser is the honest home for games like ours. They're pick-up-and-play experiences. They belong on a webpage. Putting them on Steam would be like framing a napkin sketch — technically possible, definitely awkward.

The Filipino Connection

This matters to us more than it might seem. The Philippines has one of the highest mobile internet usage rates in the world, but broadband infrastructure is uneven. A lot of our potential audience — our families, our community, the people the name "Team Sinigang" is for — plays on mid-range phones on mobile data.

Browser games load lighter than apps. They don't eat storage. They work on devices that would choke on a proper mobile game install. If you want to actually reach people, you meet them where they are. For us, that's the browser.

So What's the Catch?

Honestly? Monetisation is harder. We rely on ads — specifically Google AdSense — which means the site needs enough content and traffic for the economics to make sense. We're working on it. That's part of why we built Sinigaming and started this blog — more useful content on the site means more people find their way here, and more people finding their way here means we can keep the games free.

It's a slow build. But so was sinigang, once. Simmer long enough and it becomes something worth coming back for.

— Team Sinigang
